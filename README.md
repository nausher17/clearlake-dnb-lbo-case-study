# Independent LBO Case Study

## Clearlake Capital's Acquisition of Dun & Bradstreet

This project evaluates Clearlake Capital's announced acquisition of Dun & Bradstreet using public information available by 24 March 2025. It tests whether an illustrative five-year LBO can generate an attractive return through organic growth, margin improvement, cash generation and deleveraging without relying on multiple expansion.

## Investment question

Could the acquisition generate a 20-25% gross sponsor IRR over five years without aggressive operating assumptions or an increase in the exit multiple?

## Conclusion

Conditional approval. The illustrative base case produces approximately 2.50x gross MOIC and 20.1% gross IRR at an 8.3x exit multiple, approximately equal to the entry multiple. The case reaches only the bottom of the target range and the bear case produces approximately 7.0% IRR, so retention, normalized EBITDA, software-investment and debt-term diligence are approval conditions.

## Transaction reference points

- Announcement date: 24 March 2025
- Offer price: $9.15 per share
- Disclosed equity value: approximately $4.1 billion
- Disclosed transaction value including debt: approximately $7.7 billion
- Base holding period: five years
- Illustrative entry leverage: 5.5x adjusted EBITDA
- Base exit multiple: 8.3x

## Methodology and limitations

The principal case uses only contemporaneous public information available on or before 24 March 2025. Later merger-proxy and closing information is identified as retrospective and does not influence the entry recommendation or base forecast. The acquisition financing structure was not fully public at announcement, so all debt tranches, interest rates, fees and amortization terms are illustrative author assumptions.

The model uses reported management adjusted EBITDA as a reference but includes a recurring cash-cost reserve and explicitly flags repeated adjustments for diligence. It treats software and intangible additions as recurring economic investment rather than assuming the company is physically asset-light and therefore low-capex.

## Data sources

Primary sources include SEC filings, Dun & Bradstreet earnings materials, the merger agreement and the official transaction announcement. Every material historical or transaction input is recorded in the source register with its period, publication date, section, classification and direct URL.

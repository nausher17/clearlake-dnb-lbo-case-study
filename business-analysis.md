# Dun & Bradstreet Business Analysis

## Scope and conclusion

This is an independent public-information assessment as of 24 March 2025. The principal case uses only information published by that date. Later merger-proxy and closing information belongs in a separate retrospective appendix.

Dun & Bradstreet is a mature business-data and analytics provider with products embedded in credit, compliance, procurement, data-management, sales and marketing workflows. The asset has several qualities that can support an LBO: mission-critical use cases, subscription and API delivery, a globally recognized identifier and data set, low customer concentration, and meaningful cash generation. Those advantages do not by themselves make the investment attractive. Reported growth remained modest, North America Finance & Risk grew only 0.3% in 2024, cash investment in software and other intangibles was $208.2 million, and management's adjusted EBITDA excluded costs that require careful scrutiny. A credible return therefore depends on sustained low-to-mid-single-digit organic growth, measured margin improvement, disciplined cash conversion and deleveraging rather than multiple expansion.

## Business-quality assessment

### Products, customers and revenue model

Dun & Bradstreet organizes its solutions into Finance & Risk and Sales & Marketing. Finance & Risk supports credit decisions, supplier and third-party risk, fraud prevention, compliance, onboarding and collections. Sales & Marketing supports master-data management, CRM enrichment, prospect selection, audience activation and campaign measurement. The customer base ranges from global enterprises to small businesses and spans finance, procurement, risk, compliance, sales and marketing departments.

The 10-K identifies subscription-based online products and API-enabled delivery, while also noting transactionally consumed business-information reports. The mix is therefore not purely recurring. The strongest revenue is likely where D&B data is connected to ERP, CRM, onboarding or compliance processes; the weakest is likely discretionary campaign or transactional activity. No individual customer represented 10% or more of revenue in 2024, limiting single-customer concentration risk.

### Segments and geography

Management reports North America and International segments, and both contain Finance & Risk and Sales & Marketing. North America generated $1,672.3 million of 2024 revenue and $745.8 million of adjusted EBITDA, a 44.6% margin. International generated $709.4 million of revenue and $235.3 million of adjusted EBITDA, a 33.2% margin. Corporate and other reduced consolidated adjusted EBITDA by $54.5 million. North America supplied about 70% of revenue and International about 30%.

The geographic mix provides diversification and cross-border data breadth. It also exposes the business to foreign exchange, fragmented local competition, data-transfer rules, political changes and reliance on alliance partners. International growth was stronger in 2024, but its margin was more than eleven percentage points below North America's, leaving both an opportunity and an execution challenge.

### Retention, pricing and competitive advantage

The public materials available by 24 March 2025 did not disclose a comprehensive gross-retention, net-retention, contract-duration or price-versus-volume series. Those metrics must not be assumed. The business nevertheless shows potential switching costs because products can sit inside decision and data-management workflows. D&B's brand, D-U-N-S identifier, entity matching, historical files and global alliance network may strengthen the data set and make replacement harder.

These advantages are not absolute. Finance & Risk competes with Moody's/Bureau van Dijk, Experian, Equifax, Creditsafe and regional providers. Sales & Marketing competes with ZoomInfo, 6sense, Demandbase and numerous specialized tools. Customers can combine internal data with alternative commercial sources, and AI may make that combination easier. Pricing power must therefore be demonstrated through renewal cohorts, product usage and win/loss data rather than inferred from the word subscription.

### Cyclicality and capital intensity

Credit, compliance and supplier-risk workflows should be relatively resilient because customers need them in both good and weak economies. Sales and marketing budgets are more discretionary. A downturn can also reduce lending, trade-credit and customer-acquisition activity even if risk monitoring remains important.

The company is asset-light in physical terms but not in economic investment. In 2024 it spent $208.2 million on additions to computer software and other intangibles and $5.7 million on capital expenditures, together equal to roughly 9.0% of revenue. Treating the business like low-capex software would overstate free cash flow. The LBO model should include the full recurring software investment required to maintain data, platforms and delivery.

### Cash conversion and earnings quality

FY2024 adjusted EBITDA was $926.6 million and operating cash flow was $436.9 million. After $213.9 million of software/intangible additions and capital expenditure, simple pre-financing free cash flow was about $223.0 million before acquisitions and other investing items. Cash conversion therefore needs more diligence than the adjusted EBITDA margin suggests.

Adjusted EBITDA excludes equity compensation, restructuring, acquisition-related costs, transition costs and other items. Some exclusions may be genuinely non-recurring, but repeated restructuring and transformation spending can be economically recurring. The underwriting should maintain a recurring-cost reserve and test returns on both management adjusted EBITDA and a more conservative sponsor-defined EBITDA.

### Organic versus acquired growth

Total revenue increased 2.9% in 2024. Finance & Risk grew 2.9% and Sales & Marketing grew 3.0% in aggregate. International Finance & Risk grew 8.0%, whereas North America Finance & Risk grew 0.3%. The pattern suggests that portfolio mix and geography matter more than the consolidated growth rate. The base case should not extrapolate the fastest subsegment across the whole company.

### AI opportunity and threat

AI may increase the value of D&B's structured identity, relationship, credit and compliance data by making it easier to query, integrate and apply. It may also improve matching, onboarding automation, product development and service delivery. However, the transaction announcement's AI language is a strategic thesis rather than quantified revenue evidence. The base case should include only modest productivity and product benefits unless customer adoption and pricing are demonstrated.

The threat is symmetrical: generative tools can make alternative sources easier to combine, reduce the value of traditional interfaces, introduce hallucination and liability risk, and increase privacy, data-governance and cybersecurity requirements. Proprietary data matters, but distribution and workflow ownership will determine whether AI expands or compresses value.

## Ten investment questions

1. What are gross and net revenue retention by product, geography and customer size, and how have they changed over three years?
2. How much 2024 organic growth came from price, volume, cross-sell, new customers and product exits?
3. What proportion of revenue is subscription, usage-based, transactional, royalty or project-based, and what are the renewal calendars?
4. Which adjusted EBITDA exclusions have recurred for three or more years, and what normalized cost should remain in sponsor EBITDA?
5. How much software and data investment is maintenance rather than growth, and what happens to retention or product performance if spending falls?
6. Why did North America Finance & Risk grow only 0.3% in 2024 despite stronger third-party risk demand?
7. What explains the International margin gap, and which cost actions can close it without weakening local data coverage or sales capacity?
8. Which products are most exposed to generative-AI substitution, and which proprietary data rights can be monetized safely in AI products?
9. What share of debt and interest exposure is floating after hedges, and how much free cash flow remains under a 200-basis-point rate shock?
10. What operational improvements are already embedded in management guidance, so that the LBO value-creation plan does not double count them?

## Preliminary bull case

- Organic revenue grows 4-5% as compliance, third-party risk, APIs and international products outpace mature domestic offerings.
- Workflow integration and proprietary identifiers support retention and modest annual pricing.
- International margins improve and corporate/technology costs scale, lifting consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin toward 41-42%.
- Software investment remains sufficient but becomes more productive, improving free cash flow conversion.
- AI improves matching, onboarding and product usability and enables cross-selling without requiring large speculative revenue.
- Free cash flow pays down debt, allowing a 20-25% gross return at an exit multiple no higher than entry.

## Preliminary bear case

- Consolidated growth falls to 0-2% as mature products, cautious sales-and-marketing budgets and stronger competitors offset compliance growth.
- Price increases cause churn or down-sell, exposing weaker retention than the subscription label suggests.
- Recurring restructuring, cloud migration and software investment prevent meaningful margin or cash-conversion improvement.
- AI and alternative data weaken product differentiation while increasing governance and cyber costs.
- High cash interest constrains reinvestment and debt reduction, and an exit-multiple decline leaves limited equity value.

## What the market or seller may have misunderstood

The plausible contrarian thesis is not that D&B was a high-growth software company. It is that public markets may have discounted slow reported growth, accounting complexity and leverage more heavily than the durability of the underlying data and workflow positions. A private owner may be able to simplify the product portfolio, improve sales execution, control recurring transformation costs, strengthen International margins and make AI-enabled delivery investments away from quarterly-market pressure.

That thesis remains unproven. The seller may instead have accepted $9.15 because the required investment, competitive intensity and leverage made a materially higher standalone value difficult to support. The investment should therefore be approved only if diligence confirms retention, normalized EBITDA, maintenance investment and a path to deleveraging under a no-multiple-expansion case.

## Limitations

Public information does not provide complete retention cohorts, contract terms, product-level profitability, price-volume bridges, customer-level usage, maintenance-versus-growth software investment or the final acquisition financing structure. Those gaps are handled through explicit assumptions and sensitivities in the illustrative model, not through invented facts.

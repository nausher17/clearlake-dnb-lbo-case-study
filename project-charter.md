# Independent LBO Case Study

## Clearlake Capital's Acquisition of Dun & Bradstreet

**Status:** Approved — 19 September 2026  
**Nature of analysis:** Independent, illustrative, public-information case study

### Investment question

Using only information publicly available by the end of 24 March 2025, could an illustrative acquisition of Dun & Bradstreet at $9.15 per share generate at least a 20% five-year gross sponsor IRR through supportable organic growth, margin improvement, cash generation and deleveraging, without assuming an exit valuation multiple above the entry multiple?

The analysis will also identify the additional operating and capital-structure conditions required to reach a 25% gross IRR.

### Valuation and evidence dates

- Transaction announcement date: 24 March 2025.
- Initial underwriting date: 24 March 2025.
- Evidence cutoff: 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on 24 March 2025.
- Base holding period: five years from an illustrative transaction close.
- Currency: U.S. dollars.
- Financial year-end: 31 December.

Documents published on 24 March will retain their actual publication or SEC acceptance time where available. This distinguishes the morning announcement from documents filed later that day.

### Target return

- Minimum base-case hurdle: 20% five-year gross sponsor IRR.
- Upper target case: 25% five-year gross sponsor IRR.
- MOIC will be reported alongside IRR.
- Returns will be presented before fund-level fees, carried interest and investor-specific expenses.
- The investment must be tested with no exit-multiple expansion.

These are analytical hurdles, not representations of Clearlake's return requirements.

### Permitted sources

The principal underwriting case may use primary information publicly available by the evidence cutoff, including SEC filings, Dun & Bradstreet annual and quarterly reports, earnings materials, the transaction announcement, the merger agreement and related Form 8-K, government and industry publications, and public filings from relevant comparable companies.

Secondary sources may provide context but may not serve as the sole support for a material financial input.

### Hindsight rules

- **Contemporaneous underwriting evidence:** Published on or before 24 March 2025. Permitted in the principal case.
- **Retrospective information:** Published after 24 March 2025. Permitted only in a separately labelled retrospective appendix.
- Retrospective information may not alter the base-case forecast, entry recommendation or initial return conclusion.
- If a later document describes information that existed earlier but was not public, it remains retrospective.
- Every material input must be labelled reported, calculated or assumed.

### Required deliverables

1. Five-year illustrative LBO model.
2. Five-to-six-page investment committee memorandum.
3. One-page transaction summary.
4. Source register and data-point audit trail.
5. Public portfolio repository.
6. Accurate CV wording prepared only after project completion.

### Scope limitations

- No access to Clearlake's confidential diligence, financing commitments, investment committee materials or operating plan.
- Final financing terms are not assumed to be publicly known.
- Customer-level retention, cohort economics, pricing and product profitability may not be publicly disclosed.
- Any unavailable information will be marked "not publicly disclosed" and handled through a transparent assumption or sensitivity.
- Proposed value-creation measures are author hypotheses unless a primary source supports them.
- The analysis will not be presented as Clearlake's actual underwriting or as investment advice.

### Definition of a successful investment

The investment passes the initial test if the completed base case:

1. Produces at least a 20% five-year gross sponsor IRR.
2. Uses an exit multiple no higher than the entry multiple.
3. Relies on operating assumptions supported by historical performance or contemporaneous evidence.
4. Maintains adequate interest coverage and liquidity throughout the holding period.
5. Generates returns from a credible combination of EBITDA growth and deleveraging rather than predominantly from multiple expansion.
6. Preserves sponsor capital in the defined bear case.

A 25% IRR case will be treated as an upside requirement to be explained, not automatically adopted as the base case.

### Definition of a failed investment

The investment fails the initial test if any of the following applies:

- The base case produces less than a 20% five-year gross IRR.
- Achieving the hurdle requires exit-multiple expansion.
- The return depends on unsupported growth, margin, add-back or cash-conversion assumptions.
- The capital structure produces an unmanageable liquidity, interest-coverage or refinancing risk.
- The bear case results in material capital impairment.
- Material data gaps prevent a defensible conclusion.
